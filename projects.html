PROJECT 01 EDUCATIONAL GAME

UNITY · C# · SDG 6

Drop by Drop:
Flow of Hope.

A runner game inspired by water scarcity in rural South African communities. Guide Zuri through hazards, collect resources and help restore the community's water supply.

View GitHub ↗

01 / OVERVIEW

The idea behind the game.

Drop by Drop: Flow of Hope uses gameplay to introduce players to the importance of clean water and sanitation. Players navigate environments, collect useful items, avoid hazards and work towards restoring water infrastructure.

✦
Core objective

Connect an enjoyable game experience to Sustainable Development Goal 6: clean water and sanitation.

02 / GAMEPLAY

Key features.

  • Four-lane movement and jumping controls.
  • Collectible items and hazards to avoid.
  • Water and health values that influence gameplay.
  • Repair objectives linked to water infrastructure.
  • Tutorial prompts and HUD feedback for players.

03 / PROBLEM-SOLVING

Challenges and solutions.

A

Balancing difficulty

Challenge: Keep gameplay engaging without making it too frustrating for younger players.

Approach: Use readable hazards, clear warnings and playtesting to review resource effects.

B

Explaining objectives

Challenge: Help players understand what to collect and what to avoid while moving.

Approach: Use tutorial instructions, visible HUD values and distinct prompts.

C

Connecting the theme to play

Challenge: Make water scarcity part of the gameplay rather than just the story.

Approach: Link water collection, resource pressure and repair objectives to the main mission.

04 / REFLECTION

Process and learning.

The project combines movement, collectibles, hazards, resource tracking, interface feedback and repair objectives. Building and testing systems in smaller parts helps identify issues before they affect the whole level.

What I learned

  • Connecting mechanics to an educational theme.
  • Making HUD feedback clear during fast gameplay.
  • Breaking gameplay systems into smaller testable parts.
  • Considering age-appropriate difficulty and usability.

PROJECT 03 GAMEPLAY SYSTEMS

UNREAL ENGINE · C++ · BLUEPRINTS

The Grove Meadows.

A fantasy tower-defence game where players use defenders, resources and wave-based combat to protect a magical grove from corrupted creatures.

View GitHub ↗

A CLOSER LOOK

Inside the grove

Scroll sideways →

01 / OVERVIEW

The idea behind the game.

The Grove Meadows is a fantasy tower-defence project centred on protecting a grove. Players manage resources, place defenders and respond to enemies that move along a path towards a defended objective.

✦
Core objective

Combine resource decisions, defender placement, enemy movement, combat and wave progression into a clear gameplay loop.

02 / GAMEPLAY

Key features.

  • Build locations for strategic defender placement.
  • A resource wallet that informs build decisions.
  • Enemy variants with different combat attributes.
  • Wave-based encounters that increase strategic pressure.
  • HUD feedback for resources and match state.

03 / PROBLEM-SOLVING

Challenges and solutions.

A

Reliable enemy movement

Challenge: Move enemies through the level without relying on a navigation mesh.

Approach: Use waypoint-following and test path order, turning and arrival behaviour.

B

Target selection and combat

Challenge: Keep enemies focused on valid targets and moving when combat ends.

Approach: Define targeting rules for defenders and the tower, then test damage, death and target-loss cases.

C

Balancing enemy types

Challenge: Make enemy types feel different without making one unfair.

Approach: Tune health, speed, damage and rewards together, then test combinations across waves.

04 / REFLECTION

Process and learning.

This project combines C++ gameplay systems with Unreal Engine workflows. A useful development cycle is to build one system, test it in isolation, connect it to the game loop and verify its behaviour in play.

What I learned

  • Dividing gameplay logic into focused systems and components.
  • Connecting waypoint movement with explicit targeting rules.
  • Balancing enemy attributes as a connected set.
  • Combining code-driven systems with Unreal visual workflows.
